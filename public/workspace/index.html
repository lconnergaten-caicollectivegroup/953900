Daily execution plan

DAYS 1–5Build the sales foundation
Day 1
Align on the offer and win condition
  • Choose the one offer this pilot will sell.
  • Define buyer problem, outcome, proof, price range, and primary CTA.
  • Agree what counts as sourced, influenced, qualified, and closed revenue.
Done whenOne offer statement and written attribution rules are approved.
Day 2
Define the ideal customer profile
  • Prioritize two segments only: highest-fit buyer and best adjacent buyer.
  • Record firmographic fit, business trigger, likely decision-maker, and disqualifiers.
  • Create Tier 1, Tier 2, and nurture definitions.
Done whenThe team can explain who to pursue and who to skip.
Day 3
Build and tier the account universe
  • Identify 40–50 possible accounts from warm relationships, events, directories, and research.
  • Select the first 20 accounts using fit, timing, access, and revenue potential.
  • Identify one primary and one secondary contact for Tier 1 accounts.
Workspace proofAt least 20 leads have owner, segment, source, value, and next move.
Day 4
Prepare personalized first touches
  • Choose one real signal for each Tier 1 account.
  • Match the signal to a Fun Maps business outcome.
  • Personalize the introduction script without rewriting the entire message.
Quality checkEvery message answers: why them, why now, and why a conversation?
Day 5
Launch, rehearse, and inspect
  • Send the first 10 personalized touches.
  • Rehearse the discovery opener and objection responses.
  • Review activity logging together; correct missing next steps immediately.
Friday review10 touches logged and every account has a dated next action.
DAYS 6–12Create conversations
Days 6–7
Complete first-touch coverage
  • Send personalized outreach to the remaining priority accounts.
  • Add a same-day LinkedIn touch when appropriate.
  • Route warm-introduction opportunities to Charley.
Target20 priority accounts contacted; 100% of activities logged.
Day 8
Qualify replies
  • Respond to positive signals within one business day.
  • Confirm problem, relevance, decision role, urgency, and next meeting.
  • Move unready accounts to nurture with a reason.
Exit criteriaDiscovery stage requires a scheduled conversation and stated need.
Days 9–10
Run discovery conversations
  • Use the discovery questions; listen for business impact and buying process.
  • Capture exact language, stakeholders, timeline, and decision criteria.
  • End with a mutually agreed next move and date.
Target3–4 discoveries completed or booked.
Day 11
Send the second touch
  • Follow up with non-responders using a proof story or useful observation.
  • Do not send “just checking in.”
  • Test only one message variable at a time.
Workspace proofSecond touches and outcomes are logged.
Day 12
Run the conversion review
  • Compare reply rate by segment, source, owner, and message.
  • Keep the strongest version; revise the weakest.
  • Reassign stuck leads and remove poor-fit accounts.
DecisionOne message and one segment are named the leading hypothesis.
DAYS 13–21Prove value and create proposals
Days 13–14
Prepare and deliver tailored demos
  • Use discovery language in the demo opening.
  • Show only the use cases tied to the buyer’s priorities.
  • Confirm success measures, stakeholders, and decision timing.
Exit criteriaDiscovery is complete when need, audience, and buying path are confirmed.
Day 15
Convert demos into a decision
  • Send a same-day recap with outcomes, open questions, and next step.
  • Ask directly whether a proposal is appropriate.
  • Schedule the proposal review before sending the document.
TargetAt least one qualified proposal opportunity is created.
Days 16–17
Build outcome-led proposals
  • State the problem, desired result, scope, timeline, investment, and responsibilities.
  • Include one recommended option and one bounded alternative.
  • Define what is not included.
Quality checkEvery proposal has a live review date and named decision-maker.
Days 18–19
Run third and fourth touches
  • Use a different channel and bring new value.
  • Ask whether timing, fit, or ownership is the blocker.
  • Advance, nurture, or disqualify—do not leave leads floating.
HygieneNo untouched priority lead is older than five business days.
Days 20–21
Inspect the funnel
  • Review Prospect → Contacted → Qualified → Discovery → Proposal Sent → Closing Conversation conversion.
  • Identify the exact stage where momentum is lost.
  • Choose one corrective experiment for the final week.
DecisionOne funnel constraint and one owner are documented.
DAYS 22–30Close, codify, and decide
Days 22–24
Resolve active opportunities
  • Address objections with evidence, options, or a smaller next step.
  • Confirm decision date, procurement needs, and contract owner.
  • Do not discount without receiving a meaningful commitment in return.
Workspace proofEvery proposal has a decision date and clear blocker.
Days 25–26
Close the loop on the full pipeline
  • Send the courteous close-the-loop script to quiet accounts.
  • Move future-fit leads to nurture with a follow-up date.
  • Record disqualification reasons for message and ICP learning.
HygieneNo open lead lacks a next move or disposition.
Days 27–28
Document the repeatable motion
  • Finalize winning ICP, message, cadence, stage definitions, and handoffs.
  • Record where Charley’s involvement materially improved conversion.
  • Identify what a sales team can run without LaShea.
Operating testA new team member could follow the motion without oral instructions.
Day 29
Prepare the decision packet
  • Reconcile pipeline, closed revenue, attribution, and conversion metrics.
  • Rate responsiveness, repeatability, confidence, and adoption.
  • Complete the Day 30 decision checklist.
EvidenceThe dashboard and decision scorecard tell the same story.
Day 30
Hold the continuation review
  • Present outcomes, lessons, remaining risks, and the recommended model.
  • Choose paid retainer, revenue share, hybrid, brief extension, or stop.
  • If continuing, agree scope, economics, IP, owners, and 90-day review.
Final outputA signed next-step decision—or a clean close—is documented.

Copy-ready courtesy scripts

1. Signal-led introduction
Email · First touch
Subject: A Fun Maps idea for {{Organization}}

Hi {{First Name}},

I noticed {{specific signal}}. Fun Maps helps LGBTQ+ people discover affirming places, experiences, and community resources while giving aligned organizations a more meaningful way to reach them.

I have an idea for how {{Organization}} could use {{relevant outcome}}. Would a 15-minute conversation next week be useful?

Best,
{{Sender}}
2. Warm introduction request
Email or text · Charley
Hi {{Connector}}, I’m helping Fun Maps explore partnerships with organizations that care about LGBTQ+ visibility, belonging, and local discovery.

{{Contact}} at {{Organization}} looks like the right person to speak with about {{specific reason}}. Would you feel comfortable making a brief introduction? I can send two sentences you can forward.
3. No-response follow-up
Email · Day 3–6
Subject: Re: A Fun Maps idea for {{Organization}}

Hi {{First Name}},

One reason I reached out: {{one-sentence proof point or buyer-relevant observation}}.

If {{priority}} is on your radar, I’d be glad to share the partnership idea in 15 minutes. If someone else owns this, a redirect would be appreciated.

Best,
{{Sender}}
4. Discovery meeting opener
Call · First 90 seconds
Thank you for making the time. I’d like to understand what {{Organization}} is trying to accomplish, where LGBTQ+ audiences or affirming local discovery fit, and what would make a partnership worth pursuing.

I’ll share the most relevant Fun Maps possibilities after I understand your priorities. If there is a fit, we can agree on a next step. If not, we’ll be clear about that too. Does that work?
5. Discovery questions
Call · Qualification
• What are you trying to improve or grow right now?
• How are LGBTQ+ customers, members, visitors, or partners part of that priority?
• What have you tried already, and what is missing?
• What would a meaningful result look like in 90 days?
• Who else would evaluate or approve a partnership?
• Is there a target date or budget cycle we should plan around?
• Based on this conversation, what feels like the most useful next step?
6. Post-meeting recap
Email · Same day
Subject: Fun Maps × {{Organization}} next steps

Hi {{First Name}},

Thank you for today. I heard three priorities: {{priority 1}}, {{priority 2}}, and {{priority 3}}.

The strongest Fun Maps opportunity appears to be {{recommended use case}}, measured by {{success measure}}.

Our next step is {{action}}, owned by {{owner}}, by {{date}}. Please reply if I missed anything important.

Best,
{{Sender}}
7. Proposal cover note
Email · Decision stage
Subject: Recommended path for {{Organization}}

Hi {{First Name}},

Attached is the recommended path based on your goals around {{business outcome}}. It includes the scope, responsibilities, timeline, investment, and success measures we discussed.

Rather than review it separately, let’s use our {{date}} conversation to walk through questions and decide whether to proceed.

Best,
{{Sender}}
8. Courteous close-the-loop
Email · Final touch
Subject: Close the loop?

Hi {{First Name}},

I know priorities move. Should I:

1. Keep the Fun Maps conversation open for this month,
2. Revisit in {{future month}}, or
3. Close the loop for now?

Any answer is helpful, and there is no pressure either way.

Best,
{{Sender}}
